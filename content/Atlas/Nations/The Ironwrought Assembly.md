## Overview

The **Ironwrought Assembly** is a confederation of forge-cities ([[Hearthvault]], [[Drakthold]], [[Ashvex Hollow]], and [[Gravemire Bastion]]) and arcano-industrial settlements governed by an intricate system of guild law, mechanical invention, and shared ambition. Centered in the marshy wilderness of **[[The Gloamfen]]**, the Assembly thrives not despite the inhospitable terrain, but because of it—its founders saw in the bogs and brackish waterways both untapped resources and natural defense.

Each member-state of the Assembly elects a **Magistrate**—typically a renowned artificer, guildmaster, or war engineer—who oversees their region's representation in the Assembly's central forum. While local governance remains semi-autonomous, matters of trade, defense, innovation patents, and infrastructure fall under Assembly jurisdiction. This has led to the rise of sprawling rail systems, industrial reclamation projects, and deep mining initiatives all governed by law-code etched in steel and brass.

## Founding Structure

The Assembly was established by an accord between four emergent peoples who sought refuge from imperial conquest and ancient war: **dwarves**, **goliaths**, **goblins**, and **kobolds**. Each founding group claimed one **Magistrate Seat**, and together, these four magistrates rotate into the position of **Consul** every five years. The **Consul** serves as the primary executive and legislative authority during their term, while the remaining magistrates hold collective judicial power.

Though originally an oligarchic council, the Assembly has grown more complex over time—developing a sprawling bureaucracy, industrial courts, and a network of guild-based voting bodies that push the republic closer toward a messy but functional meritocracy.

## Culture and Society

Cultural values across the Assembly prioritize **innovation, labor, and practical knowledge** over bloodline. Even noble houses like **House Haskett** and **House Volmark** are measured as much by what they’ve built as by who they descend from. Apprenticeship is the highest social currency, and a person’s worth is often judged by the tools they wield or the blueprints they carry.

Assembly lands are a patchwork of thundering foundries, smoke-choked cities, marshborne railyards, and strange alchemical test fields. Though some outsiders see the Assembly as cold or mechanical, its citizens often display a quiet pride in their communities, bound by shared craft and purpose.

## Current Status

With the Consulship currently held by [[Bastan Volmark]] of [[House Volmark]], the Assembly is undergoing a major infrastructural expansion into deeper reaches of the Gloamfen—sparking tension with druids, Fenfolk, and nearby settlements.


## Notable Events
### The Coup of Corpuz
The **Coup of Corpuz** was a bloodless political upheaval within the Ironwrought Assembly that saw the unexpected removal of _Consul [[Jakken Corpuz]]_, a long-standing kobold magistrate, from his seat of power in 1004, 4th Age. Though his term was set to last another year, growing discontent within the kobold forge-cities —particularly among younger artificers and technocrats—fueled calls for change. Accused of stagnation, nepotism, and failing to modernize the kobold guilds’ influence within the Assembly, Corpuz found himself quietly ousted by an internal vote of no confidence orchestrated by his own constituency.

The move was spearheaded by rising star _[[Kippi Skizzik]]_, a sharp-tongued and energetic young kobold who quickly gained popularity among the ambitious lower-tier guilds. With support from key industrial factions and clever backroom alliances, Kippi was elevated to acting magistrate and soon confirmed as the new representative of the kobold states. Her ascension marked a shift in kobold politics toward innovation, ambition, and tighter cooperation with the gnomish and dwarven guilds of [[Greyharbor]].

As for **Jakken Corpuz**, he was offered a “gracious retirement” and now resides in semi-exile at a fortified estate outside of Mirewatch, technically still entitled to a pension but largely out of public life. Rumors persist that he still sends letters to the Assembly, written in increasingly erratic handwriting and rarely acknowledged. His name is quietly avoided in official records—a legacy fallen out of favor in the ever-churning cogs of Ironwrought politics.
